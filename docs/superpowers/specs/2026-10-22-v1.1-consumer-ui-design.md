# Cyber Phone Inspector v1.1: consumer UI and removal

Date: 2026-10-22. Owner: Rick Rodriguez. Status: final (revised with threat intelligence), awaiting Rick's approval.

## Intent and why

v1 proved the engine: five read only checks, a data driven rule engine, a report, a DNS monitor, all tested on a Galaxy S24 Ultra. Its screens were built for verification and look like it. v1.1 makes the app something a worried, non technical person would pay for.

The buyer suspects their phone is compromised by a malicious app that copies personal data (contacts, messages, logins, card and bank numbers) to a third party for fraud or impersonation. This is not primarily a stalkerware or domestic surveillance scenario. The buyer wants three things fast: what is on here, how bad is it, and make it go away. Then they want to know what to do about the data that may already have left.

## Goals

1. An answer in about ten seconds from one big button, with no prior setup.
2. A result that reads at a glance: a safety score and a worst first list of large cards, each saying in plain words what the app is and what it does.
3. One tap from a card to the full story and a Remove button.
4. Removal that works the only way Android allows: the app asks the system to uninstall and the person confirms in Android's own dialog. Device admin and accessibility holders get a guided two step path through the exact Settings switch, then uninstall. After removal the app rescans and shows the threat is gone.
5. A "protect yourself" list after any serious removal: passwords from another device, two step sign in, check statements, watch for sign in alerts, and for the worst cases a factory reset.
6. A "Keep it, I know this app" choice on every threat card so a family or monitoring app the person installed stops counting as a threat on future scans without vanishing from the report.
7. Rule and report wording that speaks about data theft and fraud, with the stalkerware safety advice reduced to one line inside the detail screen for the hidden app case.
8. Local threat intelligence beyond spyware: known malware, adware, scareware and banking trojans by package hash, malicious domains and IPs, and tracking and advertising kits inside apps. All lists are downloaded to the phone and checked there; the phone sends nothing. (Rick's decision 2026-10-22.)
9. A dark, clean, tech forward look (direction A from the mockups) with the safety score ring (direction C): near black, one accent colour, severity by colour, large tap targets, every control labelled for TalkBack.

## Non goals

- No change to the existing collectors, the rule condition language or the DNS monitor's mechanics. v1.1 adds two collectors (package hash, tracker scan), additive fields on the evidence model, new rules as data, presentation, flow, removal and wording.
- No cloud, no account, no analytics, no paid API. The privacy posture of v1 stands.
- No Play Store listing in this version; sideload remains the distribution.
- No iOS.

## Anti goals (a failure even if it works)

- The app ever removes, disables or changes anything without the person confirming in an Android system dialog or flipping a switch in Settings themselves.
- A kept app disappears from the report. Kept means quiet, not invisible.
- The score reads higher than what was actually checked. A check that could not run must show.
- Wording that calls something "malicious" when the evidence only says "unrecognized" or "looks odd". The hedged vocabulary of v1 stays.
- A screen that needs explaining. If a step needs a paragraph, the step is wrong.

## Constraints

- Android only, same stack and build (Kotlin, Compose, minSdk 29, targetSdk 36, R8 release, ktlint, lint fatal).
- Removal uses the `REQUEST_DELETE_PACKAGES` permission (install time, no dialog; it lets the app ask the system installer to show its uninstall confirmation). Rick approved adding it to the allowlist on 2026-10-22. The allowlist becomes eight entries; PROJECT.md, INITIAL_INSTRUCTIONS.md, the manifest and GuardrailTest change together in one commit, as before. Android still shows the person its own confirmation; the app removes nothing itself.
- CLAUDE.md, PROJECT.md privacy rules and the dependency allowlist still apply. One deliberate loosening of PROJECT.md's read only guarantee: the app may launch Android's uninstall confirmation and open Settings pages. GuardrailTest keeps its forbidden symbol list but allows `ACTION_DELETE` / `ACTION_UNINSTALL_PACKAGE` in exactly one file (the removal action), so any other use still fails the build. This is logged in DECISIONS.md as a product decision, not a drift.
- Everything the person can tap must work on the real phone (Galaxy S24 Ultra) and on the API 29 and API 36 emulators; the instrumented suite and SMOKE.md grow to cover the new flow.
- No new dependencies beyond the allowlist. Icons are vector drawables in the repo; no icon library.
- abuse.ch feeds need a free account key (`CPI_ABUSECH_AUTH_KEY` in `.env`, name in `.env.example`). It is used only by the build time fetch script on the Mac and is never bundled in the APK. Rick registers the account. Until the key exists, the build script skips those feeds and says so in the snapshot's SNAPSHOT.md, and the Known malware check reports NOT CHECKED with that reason.

## Decided (as Rick decided it, or as my call where marked)

### Screens and flow

1. **Launch.** Empty score ring, one line question ("Is anything stealing from this phone?"), two lines of what it checks, one big "Scan now" button, a quiet "What a scan touches" link. The first run consent explainer becomes a single sheet that appears the first time Scan is tapped, not a screen before Home. (My call.)
2. **Scanning.** The ring fills; the five checks tick off in plain words with their counts. The Persistence check needs usage access: if it is not granted, the step shows "needs one permission" and a button that opens the Settings page, and the scan continues without it. (My call: do not block the first result on a permission.)
3. **Result.** Score ring beside the headline ("2 threats found", "Nothing found", "1 thing to review"). Cards worst first: a coloured bar, a short label (Steals data, Hidden, Reads your screen, Can wipe this phone, Review), the app's name, and one sentence of what it does in the person's terms. A single button removes all highs when there are any. A line at the bottom: "795 apps are fine, see what was checked". The 10 minute network watch is offered here as one card ("Watch where your data goes for 10 minutes") that leads to Android's VPN consent; it is never a prerequisite. (My call.)
4. **Detail.** Label and severity, name, one paragraph of why, then four facts in a list: can read, sends to, on this phone since, runs. Then "Remove this app" (primary) and "Keep it, I know this app" (secondary). For device admin or accessibility holders, Remove becomes a two step card: "1. Turn off its control in Settings" (opens the exact page) and "2. Uninstall" (enabled once step 1 is seen done on the next check). A one line note under Remove says Android will ask to confirm.
5. **Removal.** Launch the system uninstall for the package. On return, rescan silently. If the package is gone, the card leaves the list and the score updates. If it is still there, show "Still installed" with the reason Android gives (admin still active, protected app) and the next step.
6. **After.** When the last high is gone: full ring, "Threats are gone", then the protect yourself checklist with tappable boxes. The checklist state is local only. For mediums only, a lighter version: "Review done" with no checklist. (My call.)
7. **Report.** The full REPORT.md survives behind a "Full report" entry on the result screen, with Share and Save as today. It is the professional's view; the screens are the person's view.

### Score (my calls after the builder check; Rick approved the principle that the score is earned)

- Units are apps, not findings. An app with any high finding costs 30; an app whose worst finding is medium costs 10; lows cost nothing (they are notes in the full report, not threats). Floor 0. The headline counts apps the same way: "2 threats found" means two apps with a high, "3 to review" means three apps whose worst is medium. Rick's own phone (39 lows, 6 mediums, 0 highs after tuning) therefore scores 40 and reads "6 things to review, nothing is stealing", which is honest and not alarming.
- Checks that did not run: if usage access was declined, the Persistence check could not run; the score is capped at 90 and the ring shows a grey segment with "1 check needs a permission" and the button to grant it. The network watch is different: it is optional by design, so a result without it is not capped; the ring carries a thin dotted segment labelled "data flow not watched yet" and the result screen offers the watch as a card. When the watch finishes, the app rescans on its own and the segment fills.
- The number comes from one pure function with tests: clean, one high, one app with two highs (30, not 60), mixed, kept apps excluded, usage access declined (cap 90), watch not run (no cap).

### Keep it (Rick approved; the mechanics below are my calls after the builder check)

- Keep is applied to the findings before the report is rendered, so the screens and the report never disagree. A kept app's findings become one low note; the verdict is computed after that, so a phone whose only high is a kept app reads clean on screen and in the report, and the report says why: the kept app is listed under "What this scan touched" ("You marked <app> as yours on <date>; its findings are not counted") and in the Safe list. This supersedes the earlier DECISIONS.md note that imagined a medium under Worth removing.

- Tapping Keep on a card records the package id, the rule ids that fired, and the date in the app's private storage.
- On later scans, findings for that package from those rules become a single low note: "<app> is on a monitoring list; you marked it as yours on <date>". It does not count against the score and shows no threat card.
- A new or stronger finding on the same package (a different rule, or a higher severity than kept) comes back as a threat.
- The report lists kept apps under "What this scan touched" and in the Safe list with the note above. Nothing kept is ever hidden from the report.
- A Kept apps screen in settings lets the person un keep.

### Threat intelligence (Rick's decision; the mechanics are my calls)

Sources, each downloaded to a local file and checked on the phone. Nothing about the phone's apps ever leaves it.

| Source | What it gives | Access | Refresh |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVT indicator index (Amnesty Security Lab and the lists it names, including Echap's stalkerware list) | spyware and stalkerware package ids, domains, signing certs, IPs | open | build time now; in app refresh in v1.1 |
| Exodus Privacy trackers (432 entries with code signatures) | advertising, analytics, profiling and location tracking kits inside apps | open, 572 KB JSON | build time and in app refresh |
| MalwareBazaar (abuse.ch) | SHA-256 of known Android malware with family and tags: banking trojans, adware, fake antivirus scareware, spyware | free account key | build time only (key stays on the Mac) |
| URLhaus and ThreatFox (abuse.ch) | malware distribution and command domains and IPs | free account key | build time only |

Checks added to the scan:

- **Package hash.** For every non system app, SHA-256 of its installed base APK (readable through the package's source path) compared to the MalwareBazaar set. A hit is high, label "Known malware", with the family and tag in the detail ("Banking trojan family Cerberus, tagged adware"). Split APKs: the base APK is hashed; the note in the detail says a match is on the base package.
- **Tracker scan.** For every non system app, the strings in its DEX files are searched for the Exodus code signatures (class name prefixes). The detail lists the trackers by name and category. Severity: trackers alone are a low note ("Trackers inside: 6"); five or more advertising trackers, or any tracker Exodus categorises as profiling together with a sideloaded install, is medium with the label "Adware heavy". The scan budgets this step: non system apps only, a per app time cap, and PARTIAL with a count if the budget runs out, never a stall.
- **Malicious domains and IPs.** URLhaus and ThreatFox entries join the existing domain match in the Network check; a contact is high with the label "Steals data" and the feed named in the detail.

Freshness: every list carries its date; the result screen's "what was checked" line shows the oldest list date, and the in app refresh (user triggered or on a schedule the user picks) downloads the open feeds from their pinned addresses, verifies the JSON parses and is not smaller than the bundled copy, and replaces the local files atomically. The guardrail test's single pinned URL becomes a short pinned list, still asserted.

### Wording

- Labels on cards: Known malware (package hash match), Steals data (IOC or malicious domain match, or beacon to an unrecognized host with sensitive grants), Hidden (hidden launcher with power), Reads your screen (accessibility), Can wipe this phone (device admin), Reads your messages (notification listener), Adware heavy (tracker rule at medium), Review (everything medium that is not one of those), Note (lows, shown only in the full report; "Trackers inside: N" is one of them).
- Every rule in rules.json gains a `label` and a `cardText` (one sentence, second person, data theft framing). The existing `reason` and `remediation` stay for the report.
- The remediation for highs changes in rules.json itself, so the report and the screens say the same thing: "Remove it, then protect your accounts: change passwords from another device, turn on two step sign in, check your statements. If you believe someone you know installed it, do not remove it yet; keep the phone as it is and get help." The golden reports regenerate once for this. The "single most important thing to do next" in the report follows automatically. (My call.)
- "What this scan cannot see" stays at the end of the full report unchanged.

### Look

- Dark theme by default; a light theme is not required for v1.1. Near black background, one accent (green for safe and primary actions), red for high, amber for medium, grey for notes. Severity never conveyed by colour alone: the label text carries it.
- Type large: headline 22 to 28 sp, card title 16 sp, body 14 sp minimum. Primary buttons full width, 56 dp tall.
- Every control has a content description; the result list is navigable with TalkBack in order of severity.

## Left to the builder

- Exact layout, spacing, animation of the ring, and the vector icons.
- How the two step admin and accessibility removal detects that step 1 was done (a rescan of that one collector on resume is the obvious way).
- Settings pages: Android's public intents open the Device admin apps list and the Accessibility list, not a per app page, except notification access on API 30+. The step text names the app ("find Anti Theft in this list and switch it off"). No OEM specific intents. (My call.)
- Whether the result screen uses a LazyColumn or a scrolling Column; whether Detail is a separate screen or a bottom sheet.
- The precise protect yourself wording, within the five items listed.
- Test strategy details, as long as the score function, the keep logic, the card text mapping and the removal state machine have JVM tests and the flow has instrumented tests.

## Acceptance (what done looks like)

- A fresh install reaches a result in under 15 seconds on the API 36 emulator with no setup.
- On the fixture corpus, every high and medium finding maps to one of the seven labels and a non empty card sentence (test).
- Score tests cover: clean, one high, mixed, kept apps excluded, a check that could not run.
- Removal: on the emulator, removing a planted test app leaves the list and raises the score after the system dialog; a device admin holder shows the two step path.
- Keep: a kept app is absent from cards, absent from the score, present in the report (golden test), and returns when a stronger finding appears (test).
- Instrumented tests cover launch to result, detail to keep, and detail to removal dialog.
- SMOKE.md gains the removal and keep steps for the real phone.
- Report wording tests still assert "unrecognized" never "malicious" for behavioural rules; list matches may say "known malware" because the source says so, and the detail names the source.
- Hash check: a fixture app whose hash is in a test only MalwareBazaar set is high with its family in the detail; the shipped set never contains the test hash (test).
- Tracker scan: a test APK built with a known tracker class prefix yields the tracker by name on the emulator; the per app time cap is exercised with a large synthetic DEX (test); the scan reports PARTIAL with a count when the budget runs out.
- Freshness: the result screen shows the oldest list date; the in app refresh replaces files atomically and refuses a smaller or unparseable download (tests).
- Without the abuse.ch key the build still succeeds and the Known malware check reads NOT CHECKED with the reason (test on the fetch script).
